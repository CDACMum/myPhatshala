E-commerce · dashboardsFresher

“Why do the live and final numbers differ?”

The business head sees ₹2.31 crore on the live sale dashboard and ₹2.24 crore in the next day's report and thinks the system is broken.

The interviewer is checking: can you explain speed vs batch results to a non-technical person?

Show a strong answer

The live number comes from the speed layer: it is computed within seconds, so it can't yet account for late refunds, duplicate events from retries, or orders that arrive late. The next-day number comes from the batch layer, which recomputes from all raw events once everything has arrived. Live is for decisions during the sale; batch is the audited figure.

  • Label the live figure as “provisional” on the dashboard.
  • Show yesterday's and older figures from the batch view only.

Avoid: saying one of them is “wrong”. Each answers a different question.

Ad-tech · architecture choice1–3 years

Real-time budget cap and monthly invoices

An ad platform must stop a campaign within seconds of hitting its daily budget, and send advertisers an exact monthly invoice after removing fraudulent clicks. Propose an architecture.

The interviewer is checking: can you map requirements to speed and batch paths?

Show a strong answer
  • Speed path: clicks → Kafka → stream job keeping per-campaign spend for today → stops campaigns at the cap.
  • Batch path: all clicks stored in the lake → nightly Spark job applies fraud filtering and de-duplication → exact spend per campaign → invoices.
  • Serving: advertiser dashboard shows exact spend for past days and provisional spend for today.
  • Keep the spend logic in one shared library, or use a unified engine, to limit drift.

Avoid: invoicing from the real-time counters.

Fintech · reprocessing1–3 years

A bug corrupted three weeks of metrics

A Kappa-style platform's stream job had a currency-conversion bug for three weeks. Kafka retention is 14 days. How do you fix the history?

The interviewer is checking: do you understand Kappa's dependence on retention, and the backups?

Show a strong answer
  1. Fix the job and start v2 from the earliest offset still in Kafka (14 days) into a new table.
  2. For the 7 days no longer in Kafka, reprocess from the copy of raw events archived in the lake (most teams keep one), using the same v2 code in batch mode if the engine allows.
  3. Validate v2 against known totals, then switch readers (blue-green) and stop v1.
  4. Afterwards: increase retention or enable tiered storage, and keep raw events in the lake as a safety net.

Avoid: patching old results by hand with an estimated correction factor.

Platform · strategy3+ years

“Should we migrate our Lambda platform to Kappa?”

A company runs Lambda: Spark nightly jobs and Flink streaming jobs with duplicated logic. Numbers drift every month. Leadership asks whether to move to Kappa.

The interviewer is checking: balanced reasoning about trade-offs, not a slogan.

Show a strong answer
  • Classify metrics: simple aggregations (counts, revenue) are good Kappa candidates; heavy historical analytics and ML training may stay batch.
  • Check prerequisites: log retention and cost, replay speed, exactly-once sinks, team streaming skills, schema evolution over long histories.
  • Middle path: move duplicated logic into a unified engine (Flink or Spark Structured Streaming) so the same code runs live and over historical tables in the lakehouse.
  • Migrate metric by metric, running old and new in parallel and comparing outputs before switching.

Avoid: a big-bang rewrite, or claiming Kappa is always better.
