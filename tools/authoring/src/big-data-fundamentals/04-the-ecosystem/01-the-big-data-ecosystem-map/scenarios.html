Any company · first weekFresher

“Walk me through our platform diagram”

In your first week, your lead points at a diagram with 25 logos and asks you to explain how data flows from the mobile app to the CEO's dashboard.

The interviewer is checking: can you organise tools by job and tell a clear story, instead of listing names?

Show a strong answer

Follow one record through the layers, naming the tool at each step:

  1. Source → ingest: the app sends events to Kafka.
  2. Store: a loader writes raw events into the lake (S3/HDFS), partitioned by date.
  3. Process: Spark cleans, de-duplicates and joins them nightly; Flink handles the live path.
  4. Query: results land in warehouse tables; analysts use SQL.
  5. Visualise: the BI dashboard reads those tables.
  6. Orchestrate and govern: Airflow schedules the nightly steps; the catalog documents the tables.

Avoid: reading the logos left to right without saying what each one does.

Startup · tool confusionFresher

“Can't we just use Kafka to schedule our nightly jobs?”

A product manager notices Kafka is already installed and asks why the team also needs Airflow.

The interviewer is checking: do you know the difference between moving data and orchestrating work?

Show a strong answer

Kafka moves events continuously from producers to consumers; it has no idea of “run the Spark job at 2 am, then load the warehouse, retry three times if it fails”. Airflow does exactly that: schedules, dependencies, retries, alerts and a history of every run. They solve different problems and are commonly used together.

Avoid: claiming one tool can replace every layer.

E-commerce · debugging1–3 years

At 11 am the dashboard still shows the day before yesterday

The sales head complains the dashboard is a day behind. Nobody received an alert. Where do you look, and in what order?

The interviewer is checking: can you debug a pipeline layer by layer, starting from the orchestrator?

Show a strong answer
  1. Orchestrator first: did last night's DAG run? Which task failed or is still running? Why was no alert sent?
  2. Ingestion: did yesterday's raw files arrive in the lake (count, size, partition)? Is the Kafka loader lagging?
  3. Processing: did the Spark job fail, or succeed with zero rows?
  4. Load and serve: was the warehouse table updated? Did the dashboard refresh, or is it cached?
  5. Fix, re-run from the failed task, then fix the missing alert and add a freshness check.

Avoid: re-running the whole pipeline blindly before knowing which layer broke.

Telecom · cloud migration3+ years

Moving an on-premises Hadoop cluster to the cloud

A telecom company runs a 200-node Hadoop cluster (HDFS, YARN, Hive, Spark, Oozie). Leadership wants to move to the cloud. What maps to what, and what really changes?

The interviewer is checking: do you understand the architectural shift, not only the product names?

Show a strong answer
  • Mapping: HDFS → object storage; YARN → managed Spark or Kubernetes; Hive tables → a catalog over the lake or a cloud warehouse; Oozie → Airflow (managed).
  • Real change: storage and compute separate. Clusters become temporary: start for a job, shut down after. Cost moves from fixed hardware to pay-per-use, so idle clusters and inefficient queries now cost real money.
  • Plan: migrate data in waves, run old and new in parallel and compare outputs, convert to columnar formats, set up access control and cost monitoring from day one.

Avoid: “lift and shift” of the same always-on 200 nodes into cloud VMs, which usually costs more and gains little.
