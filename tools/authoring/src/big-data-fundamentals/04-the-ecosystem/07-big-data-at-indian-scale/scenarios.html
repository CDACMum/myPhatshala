“Estimate the load on UPI”

An interviewer says: UPI handles about 24 billion transactions a month. Estimate average and peak TPS and daily event data.

The interviewer is checking: can you turn a headline number into engineering numbers, stating assumptions?

Show a strong answer
  1. Per day: 24 billion ÷ 30 ≈ 80 crore.
  2. Average TPS: 80 crore ÷ 86,400 ≈ 9,300.
  3. Peak: assume 3–5× average at busy hours and festivals → roughly 30,000–45,000 TPS to design for, plus headroom.
  4. Events: if each transaction produces ~10 events of ~1 KB across systems, that is ~8 TB a day before replication.
  5. State assumptions clearly and say how you would validate them (published peak figures, traffic history).

Avoid: giving one number with no assumptions, or designing only for the average.

System design · peaks1–3 years

Design for Tatkal-style rush hour

Your company runs a ticket sale that opens at a fixed time; last year the site crashed in the first minute.

The interviewer is checking: do you know how to protect a system from a predictable spike?

Show a strong answer
  • Estimate peak arrivals from last year and load-test above that.
  • Put a virtual waiting room and rate limits in front of the booking flow.
  • Cache read-heavy pages (availability, fares) and serve static content from a CDN.
  • Pre-scale capacity before opening time; don’t rely only on autoscaling.
  • Protect inventory with strong consistency and short seat holds; stop bots with captcha and limits.
  • Prepare switches to turn off non-essential features; watch live dashboards.

Avoid: just adding more servers to the database.

Payments · correctness1–3 years

A customer was charged twice

During a peak, a few customers were debited twice for one order after network timeouts.

The interviewer is checking: do you understand idempotency and reconciliation?

Show a strong answer

Retries after a timeout re-sent the payment as a new request. Fix: generate an idempotency key (unique transaction ID) on the client, send it with every retry, and store processed keys on the server so a repeat returns the original result. Add a status-check call before retrying, automatic reversal for failed flows, and a daily reconciliation job comparing your records with the bank or gateway settlement files to catch and refund mismatches.

Avoid: removing retries entirely, which turns network blips into failed payments.

Case study · interviewFresher

“How does Hotstar handle crores of live viewers?”

A common case-study question for data and platform roles.

The interviewer is checking: can you explain the standard pattern honestly, without claiming insider knowledge?

Show a strong answer

Explain that internal details are only partly public, then describe the standard design: video encoded into short segments at multiple bitrates (adaptive streaming), cached and delivered by multiple CDNs close to viewers, origin protected by caching, capacity pre-provisioned before big matches, graceful degradation (lower bitrate, switch off extras) and player events streamed into analytics for concurrency, quality and ad billing. Quote a published figure with source, such as the 5.9 crore concurrent peak in the 2023 World Cup final.

Avoid: inventing exact server counts or claiming to know their internal architecture.
