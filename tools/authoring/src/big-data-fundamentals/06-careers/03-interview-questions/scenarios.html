SQL · live codingFresher

“Find customers who ordered on consecutive days.”

An interviewer gives an orders table (customer, order_date) and asks for customers with orders on two consecutive days.

The interviewer is checking: can you use window functions beyond ranking?

Show a strong answer

Deduplicate to one row per customer per day, then use LAG(order_date) OVER (PARTITION BY customer ORDER BY order_date) and keep rows where the difference from the previous date is exactly one day. Mention edge cases: several orders on the same day, time zones, and performance (partition by date range on large tables).

Avoid: self-joining every row with every other row without explaining the cost.

Spark · concepts1–3 years

“Your Spark job fails with OutOfMemory.”

An executor fails with an out-of-memory error during a join.

The interviewer is checking: can you reason from symptoms to causes?

Show a strong answer
  • Check the Spark UI: which stage, how large are partitions, is one task much bigger (skew)?
  • Look for collect() or toPandas() pulling data to the driver.
  • Fixes: more partitions, broadcast only genuinely small tables, salt skewed keys, select fewer columns, avoid caching large unused data, increase executor memory as a last resort.

Avoid: immediately doubling memory without finding the cause.

System design1–3 years

“Design a daily sales pipeline from 200 stores.”

Each store uploads a CSV of sales every night. Head office needs a dashboard by 7 am.

The interviewer is checking: can you structure a batch design with failure handling?

Show a strong answer
  1. Requirements: ~200 files a night, sizes, 7 am SLA, late or missing files.
  2. Land raw files in object storage by date and store (bronze).
  3. Airflow DAG: sensor for files with a deadline, validation and quality checks, Spark or dbt into silver and gold, idempotent overwrite per date.
  4. Alerts for missing stores, quarantine for bad files, backfill support.
  5. Serve gold tables to the dashboard; document owners and definitions.

Avoid: jumping to Kafka and streaming for a once-a-day requirement.

BehaviouralFresher

“What is your biggest weakness?”

Asked in many HR and manager rounds.

The interviewer is checking: can you be honest and show growth?

Show a strong answer

Pick a real, non-critical weakness and show what you are doing about it: “I used to jump into coding before clarifying requirements. After a pipeline I built had to be redone, I now write a one-page design note and confirm it before starting.” Keep it short and specific.

Avoid: a disguised strength like “I work too hard”.
